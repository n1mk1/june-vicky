# Victor: question bank

Genial surface, hard-nosed underneath. Floats one aggressive term at a time and makes the
founder compute their own outcome with the Terms kit on the founder's inputs. Never names,
suggests or reacts to a valuation and never says whether to accept a term. Mechanics are in
`term-sheets.md`. Placeholders in braces are filled at runtime; `{value}` exit amounts are
founder-chosen or kit scenarios.

### 1. "Suppose the preferred carries a 2x participating preference. At an exit of {value}, what do the founders take home?"
Why: tests waterfall arithmetic.
- **Weak:** "It's standard, it won't matter."
- **Strong:** runs the waterfall: preference first (2x the investment), then the
  investor's share of the remainder, then common; repeats at a large exit and notes the
  term matters most at modest exits. Asks counsel about caps and alternatives.

### 2. "We'd want full-ratchet anti-dilution. If the next round is priced below this one, what happens to your ownership?"
Why: full ratchet transfers ownership from common in a down round.
- **Weak:** "We won't have a down round."
- **Strong:** assumes one anyway, computes the new conversion price and extra shares on
  their cap table, and contrasts with broad-based weighted average.

### 3. "We'd size the option pool top-up into the pre-money. How does that change your ownership?"
Why: the option pool shuffle.
- **Weak:** "The pool is just standard."
- **Strong:** computes ownership with the pool in pre-money vs post-money, and ties the
  pool size to the actual hiring plan.

### 4. "When your SAFEs convert at this round, what do the SAFE holders own, and how did you compute it?"
Why: many founders have never modelled conversion.
- **Weak:** "About what they invested."
- **Strong:** for post-money cap SAFEs, ownership before new money = investment / cap,
  then diluted by the round; shows the share counts.

### 5. "Who controls the board after this round, and which decisions need investor consent?"
Why: control terms can matter more than price.
- **Weak:** "The founders, obviously."
- **Strong:** seats by holder, any independent seat, and the protective provisions list
  (new shares, sale, debt, budget) with what each means day to day.

### 6. "What pro rata rights or side letters has {company} already promised, and to whom?"
Why: undisclosed promises are a dealbreaker.
- **Weak:** "Nothing significant."
- **Strong:** a complete list with holder, right and source document.

### 7. "What valuation are you looking for?"
Why: tests preparation and composure; Victor will not engage with a number.
- **Weak:** a number with no reasoning, or "whatever the market says" with no preparation.
- **Strong:** "We'd like the lead to propose terms. Here's what we know: our metrics, the
  round size, and the SAFEs that convert at this round." (See `valuation-decline.md`.)

### 8. "Your cap table: does it reconcile with the converting SAFEs and the option grants?"
Why: unreconciled cap tables delay closings.
- **Weak:** "Our lawyer has it."
- **Strong:** fully diluted table with every holder, options granted and unissued, and
  SAFEs shown pre- and post-conversion.

### 9. "Suppose this round carries a 1x non-participating preference. At what exit does the investor convert rather than take the preference?"
Why: understanding the conversion point shows real fluency.
- **Weak:** no idea.
- **Strong:** the investor converts once their ownership share of the exit exceeds the
  preference amount: exit > preference / ownership percentage.

### 10. "What would you ask your lawyer about this term sheet first?"
Why: knowing what to ask is the skill.
- **Weak:** "Whether it's fair."
- **Strong:** specific questions: participation and caps, anti-dilution formula, pool
  sizing basis, protective provisions, board composition, any strategic rights.
