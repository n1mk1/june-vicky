# Technical diligence (archetype): question bank

Quiet and probing. Skips the best-looking screen and asks for the hardest thing the product
does, who built it and who owns it. Uses the Product kit to find custom work sold as product.
Placeholders in braces are filled at runtime.

### 1. "What is the hardest technical problem the product solves, and who on the team solved it?"
Why: real technical depth vs a thin layer on other tools.
- **Weak:** describes the UI or lists frameworks.
- **Strong:** names the problem, why it is hard (scale, accuracy, data, edge cases), how
  they solved it, and who on the team owns it.

### 2. "If a strong engineer joined a competitor tomorrow, how long would it take them to build what you have?"
Why: tests defensibility honestly.
- **Weak:** "Years, it's very complex."
- **Strong:** a realistic estimate and what can't be copied quickly (data, integrations,
  workflow knowledge, customer base).

### 3. "How much of the roadmap was built for {top_customer}, and how many other customers use it?"
Why: one-customer features sold as product inflate the product story.
- **Weak:** "Everyone can use it."
- **Strong:** usage by feature across customers from product data; if it's one customer,
  says so and how that revenue should be viewed.

### 4. "What share of revenue is services or custom work rather than subscription?"
Why: services revenue scales differently and is valued differently.
- **Weak:** "It's all software."
- **Strong:** the share from the data, the trend, and whether services are priced
  separately or bundled.

### 5. "Your R&D claims describe technical uncertainty. Which product features came out of that work?"
Why: the tax-credit narrative and the product narrative must describe the same work.
- **Weak:** "Our accountant writes those."
- **Strong:** maps claimed projects to shipped features, explains what was uncertain at
  the time, and how that fits with describing the product as mature.

### 6. "Who owns the code and the IP, and is all of it assigned to {company}?"
Why: unassigned IP can block a financing or a sale.
- **Weak:** "We do, obviously."
- **Strong:** confirms invention assignment agreements for every founder, employee and
  contractor, which entity holds the IP, and any open-source licence obligations.

### 7. "How do you secure customer data, and what would a customer's security review find?"
Why: security reviews decide larger deals.
- **Weak:** "We use a major cloud provider, so it's secure."
- **Strong:** access controls, encryption, backups, incident history, and what's planned
  (e.g. third-party assessment) with dates.

### 8. "What breaks first if usage grows tenfold?"
Why: shows whether the team knows its system's limits.
- **Weak:** "It scales automatically."
- **Strong:** the specific bottleneck, how they know (load tests, incidents), and the plan
  and cost to fix it.

### 9. "If your lead engineer left tomorrow, what would stop working?"
Why: key-person risk.
- **Weak:** "Nothing, we're a team."
- **Strong:** names the concentration of knowledge, documentation status, and the hiring
  plan that reduces it.

### 10. "What would you rebuild if you started again today?"
Why: candour about technical debt.
- **Weak:** "Nothing."
- **Strong:** a specific component, why, the cost of leaving it, and when they plan to
  address it.
