# Thales: question bank

Warm and conversational, a former sales leader. Asks for a real story first, then the
number behind it, and checks the two agree. Ends the meeting if a founder bluffs.
Placeholders in braces are filled at runtime.

### 1. "Tell me about the last customer who churned. Who were they, and why did they leave?"
Why: founders who know their churn can tell one story in detail.
- **Weak:** "Mostly early customers on the old product." No name, no reason.
- **Strong:** a specific (anonymised if needed) customer, when they left, what they said,
  and whether the reason matches where churn concentrates in the data ({logo_churn_rate}).

### 2. "Walk me through your last closed deal: who bought, how long it took, what the objection was."
Why: shows whether the sales motion is understood and repeatable.
- **Weak:** "They saw the demo and signed."
- **Strong:** buyer role, first contact to signature in days, the objection and how it was
  handled, and whether that deal is typical.

### 3. "Tell me about the last deal you lost."
Why: losses teach more than wins.
- **Weak:** "We don't really lose deals" or "price".
- **Strong:** who won instead, the real reason as the customer gave it, and what changed
  in product or pitch since.

### 4. "How long does a new rep take to ramp, and how do you know?"
Why: ramp is where most seed hiring plans break.
- **Weak:** "A month, the product demos itself."
- **Strong:** "Our reps took about {value} months to reach steady deal volume, counted in
  closed deals per month. The plan should use that ramp, and here's the revised version."

### 5. "What share of new revenue comes through partners, and who owns the renewal on those accounts?"
Why: a channel you don't control can disappear.
- **Weak:** "The partner handles it."
- **Strong:** share of new revenue by channel, who sources and who closes, who calls at
  renewal, and what happens if the partner gets a better offer.

### 6. "Without looking: what is your {metric}?"
Why: tests whether the founders know their numbers.
- **Weak:** a guess delivered confidently, or a number that turns out wrong.
- **Strong:** the right number with its window, or "I don't remember exactly, it's about
  X, let me confirm" and then the Figure. Never a bluff.

### 7. "Who loves the product most, and could I call them?"
Why: reference customers are evidence; adjectives are not.
- **Weak:** "All our customers love it."
- **Strong:** names a reference (with permission), why they love it in their words, and
  how much of revenue looks like them.

### 8. "How much of your business is {top_customer}, and what's your relationship like at the top?"
Why: concentration risk lives in relationships.
- **Weak:** "We're very close to them."
- **Strong:** "{top_customer_share} of MRR. Our champion is {role}; we also have
  {other_contacts}. Renewal is in {renewal_month} and nothing beyond today is signed."

### 9. "Why are you the right people to build {company}?"
Why: angels back founders first.
- **Weak:** generic passion.
- **Strong:** a specific unfair insight from experience, plus what they learned and
  changed quickly since starting.

### 10. "Why do customers pick you over the alternatives? Give me three who switched."
Why: tests real evidence of winning.
- **Weak:** "Our product is better."
- **Strong:** three switch stories with the reason each gave; if win/loss isn't tracked
  yet, says so and how they will start.
