# Olivia: question bank

Expansive and energetic. Loves ambition, then makes the founder earn it with a bottom-up
market size where every input has a source, and ties the big picture back to recent
growth. Rejects unsourced top-down report numbers. Placeholders in braces are filled at
runtime.

### 1. "How big can {company} be in ten years if everything goes right?"
Why: power-law funds need outcomes large enough to matter.
- **Weak:** "Billions" with no construction.
- **Strong:** a revenue scale built from customers x price, the segments it needs, and the
  two or three things that must go right.

### 2. "Build your market size bottom-up: how many target customers, how many units each, at what price, and where does each number come from?"
Why: shows the founder has done the work.
- **Weak:** "Industry reports say it's huge."
- **Strong:** the formula with a named source for each count (e.g. a national statistics
  agency for business counts), the company's own price, and which inputs are still
  assumptions.

### 3. "Your deck says the market is {value}. Where does that come from?"
Why: a large unsourced number signals no work done.
- **Weak:** "Industry reports."
- **Strong:** "We haven't built it bottom-up yet, so we've taken it off the slide. Here's
  the method and the sources we're using."

### 4. "At the growth you've had recently, how long until you're a meaningful share of that market? What has to change?"
Why: connects ambition to today.
- **Weak:** "We'll accelerate."
- **Strong:** at {cmgr_recent} a month from {arr}, where they'd be in a few years; then
  what has to bend (channel, price, segment) and the evidence it can.

### 5. "How many customers at what price get you to a fund-returning revenue scale?"
Why: contract size x customer count is the shape of the business.
- **Weak:** no idea.
- **Strong:** shows the combinations (many small customers vs fewer large ones), which
  path {company} is on, and what it implies for sales motion.

### 6. "Which adjacent market comes next, and would the same customers buy it through the same motion?"
Why: second acts drive large outcomes.
- **Weak:** "We could sell to anyone."
- **Strong:** names the next segment or product, why the same channel works (or what
  changes), and evidence from current customers.

### 7. "Why now? What changed recently that makes {company} possible?"
Why: timing is half the story.
- **Weak:** "The market is ready."
- **Strong:** a specific shift (technology, regulation, buyer behaviour) with a source,
  and why incumbents couldn't do this before.

### 8. "What does the market look like if the biggest incumbent decides to compete head-on?"
Why: large markets attract large players.
- **Weak:** "They're too slow."
- **Strong:** where {company} wins regardless (segment, workflow, price point), with
  customer evidence, and what it would do in response.

### 9. "Year by year, what has to be true to get from here to the big version?"
Why: turns vision into a checkable path.
- **Weak:** a hockey-stick chart.
- **Strong:** yearly milestones (customers, revenue, retention) with the drivers, labelled
  as plan, and which ones the coming round must prove.

### 10. "Why are you the founders who get to the big version, not just the first version?"
Why: power-law outcomes need founders who keep scaling.
- **Weak:** "We're passionate."
- **Strong:** evidence of learning speed and of recruiting people better than themselves,
  and the gaps they plan to fill.
