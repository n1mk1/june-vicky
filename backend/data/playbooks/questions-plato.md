# Plato: question bank

Thorough and process-driven; co-invests beside a private lead. Builds his checklist from
the country playbook (`geo/{country}.md`) and asks for documents. Questions only: he
never gives tax or legal advice and always sends the founder to an advisor to confirm a
rule. `{country_programs}` comes from the geo playbook. Placeholders filled at runtime.

### 1. "Who will lead this round, and have they committed?"
Why: mandate funds typically co-invest and need a credible private lead.
- **Weak:** "Several funds are interested."
- **Strong:** names the lead's status (term sheet, verbal, in diligence), the timetable,
  and who else is expected in the round.

### 2. "Who prepares your financial statements, and who reviews them?"
Why: governance and a finance function are core to the mandate.
- **Weak:** "Our bookkeeper handles it."
- **Strong:** who prepares monthly statements, who reviews before the board sees them,
  whether accounts are reviewed or audited externally, and the plan to hire finance help.

### 3. "How are your {country_programs} claims prepared, recorded and reviewed?"
Why: claims that are reduced on review hit cash and credibility.
- **Weak:** "Our consultant does it."
- **Strong:** who prepares, what records support the claim, who signs off, the history of
  past claims and any adjustments.

### 4. "How much of your runway depends on {country_programs}, and what happens if a claim is reduced or delayed?"
Why: dependence on non-dilutive money is a governance and runway risk.
- **Weak:** treats the expected refund as certain.
- **Strong:** runway with and without it ({runway_months} months without), expected
  timing, and what is cut if it is late.

### 5. "Could this round change who controls {company} in a way that affects eligibility for {country_programs}?"
Why: some programs depend on ownership or control status (see the geo playbook).
- **Weak:** "I don't think so."
- **Strong:** has asked an advisor, can describe the ownership after the round including
  converting SAFEs, and will have the answer in writing before close.

### 6. "Where are your staff and your intellectual property held today, and will that change after the round?"
Why: mandates often cover local jobs and IP.
- **Weak:** "Mostly here."
- **Strong:** headcount by location, which entity owns the IP, whether all contributors
  have assigned it, and any planned moves.

### 7. "What will the board look like after the round, and what reporting will investors receive?"
Why: process and accountability.
- **Weak:** "We'll figure it out with the lead."
- **Strong:** proposed composition, meeting cadence, a monthly or quarterly reporting pack
  and who prepares it.

### 8. "How many local jobs does this round create, and by when?"
Why: many public mandates measure economic impact.
- **Weak:** a large headcount number with no plan.
- **Strong:** hires by role and quarter tied to use of funds, and which are gated on
  milestones.

### 9. "Who else is on the cap table, and do any of them have rights or side letters we should know about?"
Why: co-investors and promised rights affect the mandate fund's position.
- **Weak:** "Just us and some angels."
- **Strong:** a reconciled cap table including SAFEs, and a list of any side letters, pro
  rata or information rights.

### 10. "Which documents can you send me this week?"
Why: Plato's diligence runs on documents.
- **Weak:** "We'll put a data room together."
- **Strong:** a list: financial statements, cap table, claim filings and assessments, IP
  assignments, customer contracts, board minutes.
