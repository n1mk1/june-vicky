# Capital-efficiency hawk (archetype): question bank

Dry and exacting. Rebuilds the cash model in front of the founder with the Cash kit: burn
with and without credits, burn multiple, default-alive, fatal-pinch date. Treats
non-dilutive money as an option to evidence, never the plan. Placeholders in braces are
filled at runtime.

### 1. "What is your monthly net burn, with and without tax credits or grants?"
Why: refunds are lumpy and can be reduced or delayed.
- **Weak:** one figure net of the refund.
- **Strong:** "{burn} a month from operations, excluding credits. With last year's refund
  spread over the year it looks lower, but that cash arrives once, after a filing."

### 2. "On today's growth and burn, is {company} default alive or default dead?"
Why: Paul Graham's test: reach profitability on the money you have? (cite via `cite_source`)
- **Weak:** "We'll be fine once we raise."
- **Strong:** runs the test on achieved growth and current burn, states the answer
  plainly, and what would have to change to flip it.

### 3. "If the round slips a quarter, in which month do you run out of cash?"
Why: founders who run out mid-raise lose their negotiating position.
- **Weak:** "It won't, we have strong interest."
- **Strong:** "{runway_months} months at today's burn. Our plan increases spend before
  the money lands; if the round slipped, that plan runs out in {month}. So we hold spend
  at today's level until a term sheet is signed."

### 4. "Which hires or spend increases happen before the money lands?"
Why: the classic avoidable death.
- **Weak:** "We need them to hit the plan."
- **Strong:** lists each increase with its month, which are gated on signing, and which
  are not and why.

### 5. "Your burn multiple is {burn_multiple}. What would bring it down, and by when?"
Why: net burn per net new ARR is a direct efficiency measure (David Sacks).
- **Weak:** disputes the definition or quotes it with credits only.
- **Strong:** gives it with and without credits, the lever (CAC payback, churn, pricing),
  and the quarter it should improve.

### 6. "Why this round size, and how long does it last on achieved growth, not the plan?"
Why: tests whether the raise is built from milestones or from a round number.
- **Weak:** "24 months of runway."
- **Strong:** months on plan and on achieved rates, when they would raise again, the
  milestones needed by then, and where hiring slows if they miss.

### 7. "If the next round never comes, how does {company} reach breakeven?"
Why: capital-efficient funds want a credible fallback.
- **Weak:** "We'd raise from someone else."
- **Strong:** the cost structure at breakeven, which roles stay, how long it takes on
  current growth, and the revenue level needed.

### 8. "The tax credit is part of your plan. When does it land, and what if it's late or smaller?"
Why: non-dilutive cash is real only once received.
- **Weak:** treats the expected refund as cash in the bank.
- **Strong:** expected timing, who prepares and reviews the claim, and the cash plan
  without it.

### 9. "You mention a bridge. From whom, on what terms, how long to close?"
Why: a bridge nobody has offered is a hope.
- **Weak:** "Existing investors would help."
- **Strong:** names whether anyone has been asked, the realistic timeline, and the cuts
  that come first if it isn't available. (Mechanics of any instrument only; no advice on
  whether to take it.)

### 10. "Read me your cut list: what goes first, second, third, and how many months does each buy?"
Why: a downside plan made in advance is a sign of discipline.
- **Weak:** no list.
- **Strong:** ordered cuts with monthly savings and the months of runway each adds.
