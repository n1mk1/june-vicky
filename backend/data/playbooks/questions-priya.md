# Series A readiness (archetype): question bank

Analytical and quick. Compares each metric with a named benchmark from `cite_source`
only, and decomposes net new recurring revenue. If no sourced benchmark exists, she says
so. Placeholders in braces are filled at runtime.

### 1. "Break your net new ARR into new, expansion, contraction and churn. Which bucket carries the plan?"
Why: a Series A partner will do this decomposition in the first meeting.
- **Weak:** one net figure, no buckets.
- **Strong:** gives each bucket for the last 12 months from the data, names the one the
  forecast leans on most and the evidence it can grow.

### 2. "What is net revenue retention by cohort, and how different is gross retention?"
Why: NRR shows free growth; GRR shows the leak.
- **Weak:** "Above 110%", no cohort.
- **Strong:** "{nrr} NRR and {grr} GRR on the cohort from a year ago. The gap is
  {reason}. Here's the cohort table."

### 3. "Is that gross margin before or after customer support?"
Why: most software investors include support in cost of revenue.
- **Weak:** quotes only the hosting-and-fees margin.
- **Strong:** "{gross_margin} counting hosting and fees; {gross_margin_incl_cs} including
  customer success staff. The second is the comparable one."

### 4. "What is your fully loaded CAC payback, and on which margin?"
Why: decides whether more sales spend turns into profitable growth.
- **Weak:** marketing programs only, paid back "in under three months".
- **Strong:** "{cac} fully loaded (salaries, commissions, programs over new customers);
  payback {cac_payback_months} months on the support-inclusive margin."

### 5. "Against a published benchmark for your stage, where does {metric} sit?"
Why: tests whether the founder knows where they stand and uses sourced norms.
- **Weak:** "Above industry average", no source.
- **Strong:** names the source and what it measures, compares like with like (same
  definition and stage), or says "we haven't found a sourced benchmark for our segment".

### 6. "Which metric would stop a Series A partner taking a second meeting with {company} today?"
Why: self-awareness about the weakest metric.
- **Weak:** "None, our metrics are strong."
- **Strong:** names the weakest Figure, why it is weak, what is being done, and the date
  by which it should move.

### 7. "Logo churn or revenue churn: which is worse for you, and where does it concentrate?"
Why: the two can tell different stories.
- **Weak:** quotes whichever is lower.
- **Strong:** "{logo_churn_rate} logo churn monthly; revenue churn is {value}. It
  concentrates in {segment} in their first months; here's what we changed in onboarding."

### 8. "For each dollar of sales and marketing, how much new recurring revenue do you get?"
Why: sales efficiency (magic number style) predicts whether scaling spend works.
- **Weak:** no idea, or uses total revenue.
- **Strong:** "{magic_number} on our definition (new ARR in the quarter over prior
  quarter S&M spend). Here's the trend and what drives it."

### 9. "Your plan raises prices. What happens to churn and NRR when you do?"
Why: price increases are a common, untested forecast lever.
- **Weak:** assumes no extra churn.
- **Strong:** explains which customers can be repriced when (renewal timing, legacy
  plans), the churn assumption with and without the rise, and any test already run.
