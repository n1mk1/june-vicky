# Kenji: question bank

Friendly and long-term. Talks channel fit and shared customers, then floats rights as if
routine. The test is whether the founder notices what each right would cost with other
partners and future buyers. He never discusses valuation or says whether to accept a term;
see `term-sheets.md` section 7. Placeholders in braces are filled at runtime.

### 1. "How many of your customers already buy from companies like ours?"
Why: the strategic's core thesis is channel overlap.
- **Weak:** "Probably most of them."
- **Strong:** a count or share from the customer data, or "we don't track that yet; here's
  how we'd find out", plus what the overlap would mean for distribution.

### 2. "What would you need from us to grow the referral channel?"
Why: tests whether the founder has a concrete partnership ask.
- **Weak:** "Just introductions."
- **Strong:** specific asks (co-marketing, sales enablement, integration), the expected
  effect on new customers, and who on each side owns it.

### 3. "What data does {company} hold about its customers, and who owns it?"
Why: data rights are often the strategic's real interest.
- **Weak:** "We own all the data."
- **Strong:** what is collected, what customer contracts and privacy commitments say about
  ownership and sharing, and what the company can and cannot pass on.

### 4. "Our legal team usually asks for a right of first refusal if anyone offers to buy {company}. Any concerns?"
Why: trap term. A ROFR on a sale can chill other bidders.
- **Weak:** "Sure, that sounds standard."
- **Strong:** "That would let you match any offer, so other buyers may not bid. I'd need my
  lawyer to look at it before discussing. Is a notice right enough for your goals?"

### 5. "We'd want to be your exclusive channel partner in our segment. How does that sit with your other partners?"
Why: trap term. Exclusivity can conflict with existing partners.
- **Weak:** "We'd prioritise you anyway."
- **Strong:** names existing partners and contract terms affected, the share of revenue at
  stake, and what scope (segment, geography, time limit) they'd even consider discussing.

### 6. "Would a side letter giving us aggregated usage data from shared customers be a problem?"
Why: trap term. May breach customer contracts and hand data to a possible competitor.
- **Weak:** "Aggregated is fine."
- **Strong:** checks customer contracts and privacy terms first, asks what "aggregated"
  means and who sees it, and refers the letter to counsel.

### 7. "We'd like an observer seat and monthly financials. That's routine, right?"
Why: information flows to a company in the same market.
- **Weak:** "Of course."
- **Strong:** asks what information rights other investors have, whether sensitive items
  can be excluded, and how conflicts would be handled.

### 8. "If we invested, would your other distributors keep sending you leads?"
Why: signals whether the founder has thought about channel conflict.
- **Weak:** "Why wouldn't they?"
- **Strong:** share of new business from each partner, how each would likely react, and
  how the company would protect those relationships.

### 9. "How does your product fit into our customers' workflow alongside what we already sell?"
Why: product fit with the parent decides the strategic value.
- **Weak:** "It integrates with everything."
- **Strong:** specific integration points that exist today vs roadmap, and what shared
  customers actually use together.

### 10. "Where do you see the partnership in five years?"
Why: long-term thinking; also probes whether the founder would accept dependence.
- **Weak:** "You could acquire us."
- **Strong:** a partnership that grows both channels while keeping {company} independent
  and open to other partners; dependence risks named.
